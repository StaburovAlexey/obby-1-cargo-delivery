# Planar vehicle dynamics

The current driving model is an original implementation of the principles explained in [Pat Kerr’s 2D Vehicles article](https://patkerr.co.uk/2d-vehicles/). The article’s JavaScript was not copied; no open-source licence for that implementation was assumed. Existing user-owned Tow input and image provenance is recorded separately in `SOURCE-CONTROLS.md`.

## What is simulated

A rigid body moves in the XZ plane. It has mass, yaw inertia, world linear velocity and angular velocity. Four tire contact patches measure their own point velocity, including motion from rotation. Each patch resolves velocity along and across its wheel, applies rolling/engine/brake and lateral-resistance forces, and contributes both net force and yaw torque. Steering changes the front wheel directions. Steering at every input magnitude is generated by those tire forces rather than directly assigning `speed*tan(angle)/wheelbase`. The user-requested City36 rollback restores the exact Site34 model, without the City35 turn-assistance layer.

The planar model uses semi-implicit integration at 120 Hz. A bounded accumulator handles render-frame timing. The app keeps its existing 50 ms stall budget, while direct model calls permit at most 100 ms / 12 fixed steps. Excess stalled time is dropped rather than replayed as a catch-up leap. This is deliberate slow-motion during severe stalls, not a performance/FPS claim.

`fitTruckToGround` remains unchanged: it supplies vehicle height, pitch and roll from the existing road. A separate gradient callback applies the horizontal component of gravity along the grade. This is not a full 3D suspension, tire-deformation, airborne-car or rollover simulation.

## Initial calibration

All ten vehicles use the same 1,600 kg mass and 5,300 kg·m² yaw inertia. Current stock governed speeds span 11–35.4 m/s; upgrades can raise them by 6%. The complete ten-vehicle catalog is in `BIOMES.md`; a grade can shift steady speed because gravity acts separately from engine torque.

- Four wheel points: X = ±1.32 m; front Z = −1.63 m, rear Z = +1.73 m
- Lateral viscosity: 10,000 N/(m/s) per tire
- Friction-circle coefficient: 1.35
- Rolling coefficient:0.018; powered/coasting drag acceleration factors: 0.06 / 0.38 s⁻¹
- Maximum acceleration: 7.5 m/s², also limited by nominal speed / 1.8 s
- Gear-interlock braking limit: 8.5 m/s²
- Steering response: 0.22 s; low-speed wheel limit 0.48 rad
- Actual rack target is reduced by `1+(linearSpeed/9)²` to keep fast mobile turns manageable
- Collision motion is swept in pieces no larger than 0.12 m combined translation/rotation

This changes handling intentionally. In the baseline comparison, straight acceleration after 2 s is 10.59 m/s versus 11 m/s before; after 2 s of coasting from top speed, the new model retains 4.70 m/s versus 1 m/s before. The mobile steering wheel still has ±135° travel and the same gesture/deadzone logic. Holding it applies throttle; releasing coasts. Changing D/R while moving first brakes existing momentum before applying power in the other direction. There is no new brake button or handbrake.

## Road contacts and state

An inward contact impulse removes motion into the boundary and can change yaw through its lever arm. Restitution is zero; tangential velocity survives so the car can slide along an edge. Nearly parallel face contact uses two nonnegative endpoint impulses to avoid corner-selection yaw kicks. Small geometric corrections keep the existing conservative whole-body road envelope inside the route; there is no centerline snapping or branch teleport.

Per-frame `pathDistance` sums actual fixed-step center displacement, including lateral slip and reverse. Fuel and the 6 m camera-return threshold use that distance. Signed longitudinal travel drives wheel roll. Spawning, returning to the hangar, changing mode, blur and hidden-tab safety resets clear both linear and angular momentum plus the accumulator. Teleports never count as driven distance.

## Biome surfaces

`biome-surfaces.js` supplies bounded friction, lateral damping, rolling, engine and governed-speed multipliers, smoothly blended over 48 m. Grip-aware steering rack limits keep snow controllable without replacing force-generated yaw. The city pickup retains the original baseline. Ten vehicle packages have different local strengths; fuel remains actual distance × configured consumption. Volcano vibration affects visual body bob only, never the physical trajectory. See `BIOMES.md` for scope and economy results.

## BMW drift correction (2026-10-17)

The BMW forward profile now uses four contact-patch forces at its measured wheel positions. The former target-slip controller and direct yaw servo are removed. Steering rotates the front tires; lateral tire forces oppose local contact velocity and saturate in a friction circle. Rear-wheel drive shares that grip budget, and the handbrake reduces rear lateral grip while braking those wheels. There is no steering-triggered sideways force or automatic velocity alignment. Countersteering and lifting let tire forces recover the car. A tight held turn naturally loses speed instead of being forced into a permanent fixed-angle slide. Other vehicles retain their exact pre-canyon dynamics.

BMW controls use the existing reverse pedal (or S / Down). A press above the stop threshold latches handbrake until release, so slowing during a drift cannot silently engage reverse. Release and press again while stopped to reverse. Gas and handbrake can be held together. Shift remains a keyboard handbrake alias; the two extra touch buttons are removed. Safety resets clear held inputs and the latch.

Verification includes deterministic contact-force power checks, 30/60/120 Hz comparison, mirrored maneuvers, countersteering, release, actual application frame-loop driving and scoring with mocked DOM/rendering, and unchanged ordinary-vehicle trajectories. These CPU checks do not establish browser/GPU rendering or real-device touch feel.
